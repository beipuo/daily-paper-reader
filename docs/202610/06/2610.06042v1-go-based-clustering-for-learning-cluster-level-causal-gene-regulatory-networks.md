## 摘要
在高维基因调控网络（GRN）中发现因果关系在计算上极具挑战性，且由于连接密集，往往难以解释。因此，将基因归类为功能模块可以提高计算的可处理性和生物学可解释性。然而，现有的簇级因果发现方法假设可以获得预定义的容许划分（admissible partitions），并要求簇间的图结构为无环图。因此，构建此类划分具有挑战性。在本文中，我们引入了基于GO的因果发现聚类算法（GO4CD），该算法利用基因本体（GO）在多个粒度级别上构建具有生物学意义的基因划分，同时优先选择那些更有可能满足因果发现容许条件的划分。GO4CD将参与共享生物过程的基因归为一组，并通过本体层次结构传播基因注释，以实现不同粒度的划分。此外，我们将GO4CD与簇上因果学习（CLOC）算法相结合，并在条件独立性预言机（oracle）以及使用多元条件独立性检验的模拟基因表达数据上，评估了对真实马尔可夫等价类的恢复能力。我们在多个大肠杆菌（E. coli）调控子网络上对GO4CD进行了评估，发现其不可容许的情况仅占18.1%，而基于语义相似度的基准方法则高达65.3%-82.3%。我们的结果表明，GO4CD在学习基于生物学意义基因簇定义的因果GRN方面具有显著优势。

## Abstract
Discovery of causal relationships in high-dimensional Gene Regulatory Networks (GRN) is computationally challenging and often difficult to interpret due to dense connections. Therefore, grouping genes together into functional modules can improve tractability and biological interpretability. However, existing cluster level causal discovery methods assume access to a predefined admissible partitions, requiring the graph over clusters to be acyclic. Constructing such partitions is therefore challenging. In this work, we introduce GO-based Clustering for Causal Discovery (GO4CD), an algorithm that uses Gene Ontology (GO) to construct biologically meaningful gene partitions at multiple levels of granularity, while favoring those more likely to be admissible for causal discovery. GO4CD groups together genes participating in a shared biological process, and propagates gene annotations through the ontology hierarchy to achieve different granularity of partitions. Furthermore, we integrate GO4CD with Causal Learning over Clusters (CLOC) algorithm and evaluate recovery of true Markov equivalence class both with an oracle of conditional independencies and on simulated gene expression data using multivariate conditional independence tests. We evaluate GO4CD on multiple E.coli regulatory subnetworks and find that it is inadmissible in 18.1% of the cases, compared with 65.3-82.3% for the semantic-similarity baselines. Our results indicate that GO4CD is substantially better suited to learning causal GRNs defined over biologically meaningful gene clusters.
