## 摘要
在化学反应网络分析中，识别支持多稳态（multistationarity）和多重稳定性（multistability）的动力学参数及守恒量仍然具有挑战性。结构理论虽然提供了存在性和排除性结果，但并不总能给出相应参数区域的显式且可解释的描述。我们提出了一种计算与机器学习框架，用于发现并表征质量作用系统中具有多稳态和多重稳定性数值证据的参数区域。我们将这些数值检测到的行为称为近似多稳态和近似多重稳定性。该框架结合了拉丁超立方采样、自适应采样和可解释的代理模型。逻辑回归和神经网络代理模型分别在积累的检测标签上进行训练，并独立引导后续采样向具有高近似多稳态或多重稳定性得分的区域移动。边界细化旨在针对已检测和未检测数值类别之间的转换。随后，利用白金汉-派（Buckingham-Pi）群与 CN2 规则归纳法，获得所检测区域的可解释经验描述。说明性网络得出的规则与已知多稳态条件一致，而 Schlögl 模型展示了多稳态检测的自适应增强。在混合组氨酸激酶模型和顺序双磷酸化循环中，在相同的采样预算下，与全局采样相比，自适应采样显著增加了检测到多重稳定性的参数-总量对的数量。该框架通过提供高效、参数解析的多稳态和多重稳定区域的发现与表征，补充了分析化学反应网络理论。

## Abstract
Identifying kinetic parameters and conserved quantities that support multistationarity and multistability remains challenging in chemical reaction network analysis. Structural theory provides existence and exclusion results, but does not always yield explicit, interpretable descriptions of the corresponding parameter regimes. We present a computational and machine-learning framework for discovering and characterizing parameter regimes with numerical evidence of multistationarity and multistability in mass-action systems. We refer to these numerically detected behaviors as approximate multistationarity and approximate multistability. The framework combines Latin hypercube sampling, adaptive sampling, and interpretable surrogate modeling. Logistic-regression and neural-network surrogates are trained separately on accumulated detection labels and independently guide later sampling toward regions with high scores for approximate multistationarity or multistability. Boundary refinement targets transitions between detected and undetected numerical classes. Buckingham--Pi groups are then used with CN2 rule induction to obtain interpretable empirical descriptions of detected regimes. Illustrative networks yield rules consistent with known multistationarity conditions, while the Schlögl model demonstrates adaptive enrichment of multistationarity detections. In the hybrid histidine kinase model and the sequential dual phosphorylation cycle, adaptive sampling substantially increased the number of parameter - total pairs with detected multistability relative to global sampling under equal sampling budgets. The framework complements analytical chemical reaction network theory by providing efficient, parameter-resolved discovery and characterization of multistationary and multistable regimes.
